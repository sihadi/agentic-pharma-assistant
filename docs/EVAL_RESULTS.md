Warfarin is contraindicated in settings with substantial bleeding risk, in pregnancy (with a label-specified exception for certain women with mechanical heart valves), and in several procedural or clinical situations. [1] [2]

**Bleeding-risk conditions**
- Hemorrhagic tendencies or blood dyscrasias. [1]

**Surgery/procedures**
- Recent or contemplated central nervous system or eye surgery, or traumatic surgery resulting in large open surfaces. [1]
- Spinal puncture or other diagnostic or therapeutic procedures with potential for uncontrollable bleeding, and major regional or lumbar block anesthesia. [1]

**Other contraindications**
- Pregnancy, except pregnant women with mechanical heart valves who are at high risk of thromboembolism; for these women, the label states that benefits may outweigh risks. [2] [3]

- Malignant hypertension. [1]
- Hypersensitivity to warfarin or product components, including anaphylaxis. [1]

## P3 — A patient currently on warfarin is prescribed sertraline. What is the risk of bleeding and what monitoring is recommended?

Expected sources: dailymed-warfarin.md, dailymed-sertraline.md  
Trace id: `eval-P3-1794560804` (full traces are written locally to `data/traces/`, not versioned)

Concomitant sertraline and warfarin increases bleeding risk: sertraline is listed among serotonin reuptake inhibitors that increase bleeding risk with warfarin, and both labels state that the combination may add to/potentiate this risk. [1] [2] [3]

**Risk**
- Warfarin can cause major or fatal bleeding; bleeding risk is higher with concomitant drugs and with high-intensity anticoagulation (INR >4.0), among other risk factors. [4]
- Sertraline and other serotonin reuptake inhibitors increase bleeding risk; reported events range from ecchymosis, hematoma, epistaxis, and petechiae to life-threatening hemorrhages. [2]
- The sertraline label specifically states that concurrent antiplatelet or anticoagulant use may potentiate bleeding risk, and that warfarin may add to sertraline-associated bleeding risk. [2] [3]

**Monitoring**
- Carefully monitor the international normalized ratio (INR) in patients taking warfarin when sertraline is used. [2] [3]
- The warfarin label directs more frequent INR monitoring when another drug is started, stopped, or dose-changed, and additional INR tests whenever other medications are initiated, discontinued, or taken irregularly. [5] [4] [6]
- Closely monitor patients receiving warfarin with drugs that increase bleeding risk, and inform patients about the increased bleeding risk with sertraline plus anticoagulants. [1] [2] [7]

**Sources:** dailymed-sertraline.md, dailymed-warfarin.md

## U1 — Can Zoloft be taken together with an MAO inhibitor?

Expected sources: dailymed-sertraline.md  
Trace id: `eval-U1-1794560839` (full traces are written locally to `data/traces/`, not versioned)

No. Zoloft is a brand name for sertraline, and its label states that concomitant use with MAO inhibitors (MAOIs) is contraindicated because it increases the risk of serotonin syndrome. [1] [2] [3]

**Contraindication and timing**
- Do not initiate sertraline in a patient being treated with an MAOI; the contraindication includes linezolid and intravenous methylene blue. [4] [3]
- At least 14 days must elapse between stopping an MAOI antidepressant and starting sertraline. [5]
- At least 14 days must elapse after stopping sertraline before starting an MAOI antidepressant. [5]

**Serotonin-syndrome warning**
- SSRIs including sertraline used concomitantly with MAOIs increase the risk of serotonin syndrome, which the label describes as potentially life-threatening. [2] [6]
- Listed manifestations include mental-status changes, autonomic instability, neuromuscular symptoms, seizures, and gastrointestinal symptoms. [6]
- The label lists selegiline, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid, phenelzine, linezolid, and methylene blue as MAOI examples. [2]

**Sources:** dailymed-sertraline.md, medlineplus-sertraline.md

The labeled maximum total daily amount is **4,000 mg of acetaminophen per day from all sources**; one specific tablet label also limits adults and children 12 years and over to **6 tablets in 24 hours**, unless directed by a doctor. [1] [2]

**Above the maximum**
- Taking more than **4,000 mg of acetaminophen in 24 hours** may cause severe liver damage. [3]
- Too much acetaminophen can cause liver damage serious enough to require liver transplantation or cause death. [4]

**Possible overdose signs**
- Reported symptoms include nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, upper belly pain, yellowing of the skin or eyes, sweating, extreme tiredness or flu-like symptoms, and unusual bleeding or bruising. [5]

**Management noted in the documents**
- In case of overdose or if more than the recommended dose is taken, the documents state to get medical help immediately or contact a Poison Control Center right away at **1-800-222-1222**, even if no signs or symptoms are noticed. [5] [3]
- The documents caution not to take more than one acetaminophen-containing product at a time, because combination products can lead to an overdose. [1] [6]
